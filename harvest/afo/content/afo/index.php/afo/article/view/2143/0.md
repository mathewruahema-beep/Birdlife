### 

Black Falcons *Falco subniger* breeding near Mackay in coastal Queensland

 Marlis Schoeb, Robert Werner, Heather Janetzki, S. J.S. Debus
pp. 40-46

### Abstract

The breeding of 1–2 pairs of Black Falcons *Falco subniger* in central-coastal Queensland was observed in 2015 and more extensively in 2017. After disturbance to one site in late 2015 resulting in the Falcons leaving that area, two pairs nested 700 m apart at another site in 2017, one pair raising four fledglings and the other raising three. One female was strongly defensive of nestlings and fledglings, swooping and calling at observers on the ground below. Both adult males had a pale forehead and face, and one juvenile from each brood also had pale ear-coverts. Some aspects of breeding biology and behaviour are described. From pellets and pellet fragments (*n* = 51, including 46 whole pellets) and orts (*n* = 8 prey animals), collectively representing at least 19 prey animals, the Falcons’ breeding diet consisted of mammals (63% by number, mainly rodents) and birds (37%), with mammals contributing perhaps two-thirds of prey biomass. One pair reoccupied and copulated at one of the 2017 nest-sites in July 2018, but the other pair was not detected near its 2017 nest, and no further breeding activity at either nest was observed up to September 2018.
