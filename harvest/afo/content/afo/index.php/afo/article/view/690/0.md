### 

Nesting of the Regent Honeyeater *Xanthomyza phrygia *near Armidale, New South Wales

 Andrew J Ley, M B Williams
P. 328-336

### Abstract

Four nests of Regent Honeyeaters *Xanthomyza phrygia *were studied during the 1993 breeding season and behaviour was documented quantitatively. Regent Honey eaters showed breeding behaviour that is typical of many honey eaters. The female alone collected nesting material and built the nest, although the male usually accompanied her as she did this. The female alone incubated, for about 69% of daylight hours in bouts averaging 10.5 minutes. Both parents fed the nestlings at visitation rates up to 76.8 per hour, amongst the highest recorded for any bird species. The female alone brooded the nestlings during the day; the frequency of brooding decreased as the nestling period advanced. Both parents continued to feed the young after fledging, at rates of up to 60 times per hour. Both parents defended the nest tree against all species of birds, although the male was involved in more aggressive encounters than the female at all stages, but especially during incubation. The aggressiveness of Regent Honeyeaters during breeding may be partly to defend against the theft of nesting material by other birds. Other aspects of behaviour observed in breeding populations of Regent Honeyeaters included a nest-site selection display, sunning, anting and vocal mimicry.
