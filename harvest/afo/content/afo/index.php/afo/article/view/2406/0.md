### 

Targeted bioacoustic surveys detect Plains-wanderers *Pedionomus torquatus* in Melbourne’s Western Grasslands

 Aaron Grinter, Chris Hartnett, Kathleen Armstrong

### Abstract

The Plains-wanderer *Pedionomus torquatus* is a small, Critically Endangered Australian grassland bird, the sole representative of the Pedionomidae family. Historically, it was regularly encountered in native grasslands around Melbourne, Victoria, but declined because of habitat loss, caused primarily by rapid urban growth, habitat degradation and conversion to broad-acre cropping. Consequently, the species has not been formally recorded in these grasslands in >15 years. Recent habitat assessments identified grasslands that are potentially suitable for Plains-wanderers. Therefore, our study aimed to determine whether the species continues to occur in remaining grassland habitat close to Melbourne. Between 2022 and 2025, we installed a network of bioacoustic monitoring sites (*n* = 44), which recorded for 1 hour each side of dawn and dusk (4 hours per day). Recordings were analysed using two automated recogniser approaches: a template pattern-matching algorithm and Google’s Perch machine-learning model. Plains-wanderers were detected by the bioacoustic recorders at four sites, across two locations, over 3 years. Our results show that where native grassland habitat is maintained within a suitable range for Plains-wanderers (sparse, open, predominantly native grassland with ~50% bare ground and low grass cover 10–15 cm high), the species may continue to utilise these grasslands, even in urban-adjacent areas. These findings have implications for the ongoing protection and management of native grasslands on the Victorian Volcanic Plains, particularly within the proposed Western Grassland Reserve.
