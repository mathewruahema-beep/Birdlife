### 

Roost-site fidelity and post-fire foraging in the nomadic Ground Cuckoo-shrike *Coracina maxima* in the Kimberley

 Thomas D. J. Sayers, Tommy L. Landmann

### Abstract

The arid to semi-arid interior of Australia supports a high proportion of nomadic bird species, yet their movements and behavioural ecology remain poorly understood. The Ground Cuckoo-shrike *Coracina maxima* is among the least studied of these species, particularly in northern Australia. Here we document the site persistence, movement patterns and habitat use of two focal Ground Cuckoo-shrikes from June to September 2025 at the Charnley River–Artesian Range Wildlife Sanctuary in the western Kimberley, Western Australia, at the edge of the species’ range and centred on a fixed roost site. Through field observation and bioacoustic monitoring, we recorded ≥41 consecutive days of roost-site residency (potentially ≥73 days), repeated use of a single roost tree for ≥23 days, and predictable diel movement patterns involving departures from and returns to the roost site at sunrise and sunset, respectively. Ground foraging was observed in recently burnt habitat, with consistent flight paths between the roost site and nearby early dry season burn scars, indicating that roost-site fidelity may be associatedwith post-fire resource availability. The subsequent arrival of a third individual coincided with changes to vocal activity and social behaviour and might have ultimately influenced the departure of the two focal birds. These findings demonstrate the
 structured spatio-temporal organisation of a rarely encountered nomadic species whilst highlighting the potential influence of
 fire management on these dynamics.
