### 

Observations on Parent/Young Behaviour in a Pair of Black-shouldered Kites *Elanus axillaris*

 David G Read
P. 109-121

### Abstract

A nest of Black-shouldered Kites *Elanus axillaris *on the edge of an urban setting near Bathurst, New South Wales, in August-September 1999 enabled observation of parent/young relations from before fledging through to independence and the parents laying a second clutch. The diet was entirely House Mice *Mus domesticus. *The rate at which the parents fed the chicks averaged 1.9 items/h (n = 11.5 h) pre-fledging and 2.2 items/h (n = c. 8.5 h) post-fledging. The postfledging period of dependence on parental feeding lasted 21 days, when the adults began a second nest. Young stayed in the natal territory for a further 7 days. Some aspects of parental care, prey transport, and independence are new for the species and perhaps for Australian raptors. Also reported are details of nests, copulations, and interspecific synergistic and agonistic interactions.
