### 

The Juvenile Food-begging Calls and Associated Aspects in the Australian 'Broad-tailed' (Platycercine) Parrots

 John Courtney
P. 169-184

### Abstract

The juvenile food-begging calls of representative species of seven of the II traditional genera of 'broad-tailed' (platycercine) parrots from Australia were studied and compared with those of other Australian parrots and cockatoos. Three subgroups were defined. One subgroup *(Melopsittacus, **Pezoporus) , *thought to be the most 'primitive', possesses a pure-sounding quavering whistle which remains unchanged with age of chick. The two other subgroups *(Neophema *and the 'advanced' platycercines) have an age-related, progressively changing juvenile food-begging call. This is atypical of other Australian parrots and cockatoos, all of which have an unchanging begging call until it is discarded on independence. It is concluded that members of this large group traditionally known as the 'broad-tailed' parrots are more closely related to one another than to other parrots, and therefore are monophyletic even though the group may be of considerable antiquity.
