### 

Foraging and Breeding Behaviour of the Australian Kestrel *Falco cenchroides *on the Northern Tablelands of New South Wales

 David Paull
P. 85-92

### Abstract

A breeding pair of Australian Kestrels *Falco cenchroides *was observed for six weeks (105 hours), September-November 1988 at Armidale, N.S.W. Breeding behaviour, sex roles and foraging behaviour are described. In the pre-incubation period, both sexes hunted: male by hovering, female by perch hunting, with attack rates of 3.8 and 2.5 per hour and success rates of 76% and 78% respectively. In the incubation period, the male made at least 0.5 captures per hour and delivered 0.4 prey items per hour to the female. In the early nestling period, male and female each made 0.3 deliveries per hour to the nest. The diet was 83% invertebrates, 17% skinks and (1% mammals by number: entirely invertebrates in the pre-incubation period, entirely skinks in the incubation period and a mixed diet (predominantly vertebrates) in the nestling period. Overall, invertebrates contributed the most by biomass (68%), but vertebrates predominated by weight in the incubation and nestling periods (98%) .
