### 

Breeding biology and behaviour of a pair of Blackbreasted Buzzards *Hamirostra melanosternon* near Alice Springs, Northern Territory, including response to nest destruction

 Peter J Nunn, Chris R Pavey
pp. 57-76

### Abstract

 A breeding pair of Black-breasted Buzzards *Hamirostra melanosternon* was observed for 98.4 h between August 2011 and January 2012 near Alice Springs, Northern Territory, covering the cycle from nest-building to the fledging of two chicks. Although both parents (which were individually recognisable) made similar contributions to nest-building, mainly the female tended the nest during the incubation and nestling phases (female performed 93% of daytime incubation, 84% of nestling attendance), and the male hunted and brought food during this time. Thus, the Buzzards’ parental behaviour was similar to that of the closely related Square-tailed Kite *Lophoictinia isura*. The Buzzard pair’s laying interval for a clutch of two eggs was estimated as 8–13 days, the incubation period as 32–38 days, and the nestling period as 68–73 days. We describe for the first time courtship feeding and post-laying copulation. After the original nest-tree was burnt and collapsed early in the incubation phase, the Buzzard pair successfully re-nested ~3.5 km away.
