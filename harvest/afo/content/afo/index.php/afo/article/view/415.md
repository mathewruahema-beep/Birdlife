### 

The food and foraging rate of an Australasian Bittern

 Peter Menkhorst
p. 133-142

### Abstract

Extended observations and photography of an Australasian Bittern *Botaurus poiciloptilus* as it foraged during daylight hours along channel banks indicated that its primary prey species was the Growling Grass Frog *Litoria raniformis*, a large terrestrial frog associated with permanent waterbodies in south-eastern Australia. The Bittern was observed to capture 17 Growling Grass Frogs at a mean rate of one every 21 minutes of hunting time. The only other prey item recorded was a single Common Galaxias *Galaxias maculatus*, a small indigenous fish. The hunting behaviour of the Bittern and its manner of preparing prey for swallowing are described. All frogs were repeatedly dunked in water before being swallowed, presumably to remove distasteful mucus from their skin. Both the Australasian Bittern and the Growling Grass Frog are listed nationally as threatened species. Given the predation rate observed, the predator–prey relationship between these two threatened species may produce an interesting management conundrum.
