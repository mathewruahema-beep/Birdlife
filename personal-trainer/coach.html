Your program

Your week, by the evidence

Strength: 3 sessions, every weekAfter 50 you lose 1–2% of muscle a year unless you load it. Resistance training is the only thing that reverses that. Leave 1–2 reps in the tank; add weight or reps when the top of the range feels easy.
Zone 2: 150 minutes a week minimumWalking, cycling, rowing at a pace where you can talk but not sing. Cardiorespiratory fitness is the strongest single predictor of how long you live. Rest days are walk days.
Intervals: one session a week (from week 4)4 × 4 minutes hard, 3 minutes easy between. This is the fastest known way to raise VO2max. Build the Zone 2 base for three weeks first.
Power and balance are built inSwings, squat jumps, split squats and carries. Leg power and single-leg balance are what keep a 70-year-old off the floor. You are training for that now.

Daily targets

A day that hits those numbers

What the science says at 56

Protein: 35–40 g per meal, three to four times a dayOlder muscle needs a bigger dose per meal to trigger growth (anabolic resistance). Two small serves don't add up to one proper one. Eggs, meat, fish, dairy, legumes.
Creatine monohydrate, 3–5 g every dayOne of the best-evidenced supplements there is for strength and lean mass in men over 50 who lift. Cheap, safe for healthy kidneys. Check with your GP if you have kidney issues.
Fibre 30 g, vegetables at every mealBowel health, blood sugar, blood pressure. Kūmara, oats, beans, greens.
Alcohol: 10 standard drinks a week, never more than 4 in a dayThat's the Australian guideline. Beer and sugary drinks also drive gout, and Māori men carry a higher gout risk. Every drink is roughly one lost training set.
Sleep 7–9 hoursUnder 6 hours and your body burns muscle instead of fat when you diet, and your appetite hormones fight you all day.

Bodyweight

Waist (the number that matters more)

Men: under 94 cm is the target. 94–102 cm means raised risk of heart disease and type 2 diabetes; over 102 cm is high risk. Measure monthly, relaxed, at the belly button.

Voice coach

Deadpan Kiwi talks like a dry, self-aware mate with te reo Māori woven through, and still counts your missed sessions. Te reo words are respelled for the speech engine so they sound right. Straight coach is the plain version.

Get a Kiwi voice on your Samsung

The app can only use voices your phone has installed. Do this once, then come back and pick it above.

Google voice (works in Chrome): Settings → General management → Text-to-speech → Preferred engine: Speech Services by Google → tap the gear → Install voice data → English (New Zealand). Download it on Wi-Fi.

Samsung voice (works in Samsung Internet): same screen → Preferred engine: Samsung text-to-speech engine → gear → Install voice data → English (New Zealand) if it is offered; Samsung does not ship one on every model. If it is missing, use the Google engine and open this app in Chrome.

After installing, tap Refresh voice list. Kiwi voices show with a ★; the smoother network voices are marked smooth and need data or Wi-Fi to speak.

Uses your phone's built-in speech. On Samsung, an Australian English voice is picked automatically if installed (Settings → General management → Text-to-speech).

Your Galaxy Watch

Today: your watch feeds Samsung Health; the export on the Progress tab brings that into the coach (steps, sleep, heart rate, weight, workouts). Do the export once a week, Sunday night, as part of the review.

Live heart rate in a session: open this app in Chrome (not Samsung Internet) and tap Connect heart rate on the session screen. A chest strap works straight away. The watch itself only shows up if it is running a heart-rate broadcast app from the Galaxy Store; Samsung does not broadcast by default.

Automatic sync (no export step) needs a native Android app reading Health Connect. That is phase 2 and a separate build.
