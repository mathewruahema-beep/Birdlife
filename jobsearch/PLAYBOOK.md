# Remote Application Playbook

For Mathew Hema. How to get a remote senior IT role from Melbourne, in the order the work
happens. Every section ends with what to do this week. The board and the tool implement
most of it; this document is the reasoning behind them.

## 0. What a remote employer is buying

A remote hire at Head of IT or CIO level is a trust decision made on thin evidence. The
hiring manager has three private questions, and every part of your application should answer
one of them:

1. **Can this person deliver without being watched?** Evidence: outcomes with numbers,
   programmes you owned end to end, and any period where you led people you did not sit with.
2. **Can we reach them when it matters?** Evidence: a stated working window in their time
   zone, a history of working across time zones, and a plain statement of your set-up.
3. **Will they stay?** Evidence: tenure that makes sense, a reason for wanting remote that is
   about the work, and a career pattern that fits the role.

Your resume already has the strongest possible answer to question one (Crawford Asia Pacific,
reporting to a Global CIO in the USA with teams in the UK and India, for fourteen years). It
is buried in the eighth role on page two. The single biggest change to make is to move that
evidence to the top and say the word "remote" in the first five lines.

**This week:** adopt the remote master resume in `resume/` and put the remote statement in
the header of every version you send.

## 1. Positioning

### The remote statement

One line, in the resume header, on LinkedIn, and in the first paragraph of every letter:

> Melbourne, Australia. Remote. AEST/AEDT with daily overlap to APAC, US Pacific mornings and
> UK afternoons. Fourteen years leading distributed teams across Australia, the USA, the UK
> and India.

### The pattern statement

Employers hire senior people for a pattern, not a list. Yours is real and repeatable:

> I inherit fragmented or under-invested ICT functions, establish governance and a security
> baseline, rebuild the team and vendor model, then reduce cost while lifting service levels.

Use it verbatim. Then prove it three times with numbers (165 to 1,000 staff at Crawford with
10 percent cost reduction year on year; Essential Eight Maturity Level 3 in two regulated
businesses; the lending platform from inception to live at Good Shepherd).

### What to cut

- The interests line. It costs space and answers none of the three questions.
- "[LinkedIn URL]" placeholder. Either the real URL or nothing.
- Venue AV and hybrid-event delivery at Independent Schools Victoria. It reads as on-site.
- Anything that lists responsibilities without a result.

### Titles to apply for

Your realistic remote band is Head of IT, Head of Technology, IT Director, IT Operations
Manager, Senior IT Manager, Information Security Manager, and CIO at organisations up to
about 500 staff. Below that band (IT Team Lead, Systems Administrator) you will be screened
out as overqualified and the pay will not clear your floor. Above it (CIO at 2,000 staff plus)
remote is rare and the search will take a year.

## 2. Where remote roles for your profile actually come from

Four lanes, in order of expected yield for a Melbourne-based senior IT leader.

| Lane | What it looks like | Hours | Yield | Where |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Australian employers outside Melbourne with a remote policy | Sydney, Brisbane, Canberra, Perth head offices; NFPs, member bodies, mid-size financial services, health, education | AEST, occasional travel | Highest | SEEK, LinkedIn, EthicalJobs, Adzuna, recruiters |
| B. New Zealand employers | NZ NFPs, councils, mid-size businesses hiring across the Tasman | NZST is AEST plus 2 | Medium | SEEK NZ, TradeMe, LinkedIn |
| C. APAC regional roles at multinationals | "IT Manager APAC", "Regional IT Lead", security and compliance managers covering the region | AEST with Singapore/India overlap and US calls at 7am or 10pm | Medium | LinkedIn, company boards (Greenhouse, Lever, Workable), recruiters |
| D. Global remote-first companies | Tech companies with "anywhere in APAC" roles; often IT, security or business systems management | Mostly async, some US Pacific afternoon overlap (your 8am to 11am) | Low but well paid | Himalayas, Remotive, company boards, Working Nomads |
| E. Fractional and interim | 3 to 12 month CIO or Head of IT engagements, 1 to 3 days a week, often fully remote | Flexible | Medium, faster to start | Technology Leaders Australia, recruiters, your own network |

Lane A is where most of your applications should go. Lane D is where the tool's global
boards look, and it is worth watching, but do not let its volume set your expectations.

### Time zone maths from Melbourne

| Location | Offset from Melbourne (AEST) | Their 9am to 5pm in your time |
|---|---|---|
| Sydney, Brisbane, Canberra | 0 (Brisbane is minus 1 in summer) | Same day |
| Perth | minus 2 | 11am to 7pm |
| Auckland | plus 2 | 7am to 3pm |
| Singapore, Manila | minus 2 | 11am to 7pm |
| India (Bengaluru) | minus 4.5 | 1.30pm to 9.30pm |
| London | minus 9 (minus 10 in AEDT) | 6pm to 2am |
| US Pacific | minus 17 (varies with daylight saving) | 2am to 10am, so their afternoon is your early morning |
| US Eastern | minus 14 | 11pm to 7am |

A US employer that wants four hours of daily overlap is asking for your 6am to 10am. Say yes
to that only if the money and the role justify it. A UK employer's morning is your evening;
their afternoon is your night. UK roles rarely work from Melbourne.

**This week:** set lane A as the default filter in your head. Run `links` and work the SEEK,
LinkedIn, EthicalJobs and Adzuna searches by hand until the automated sources have had their
first run.

## 3. The application itself

### ATS hygiene

Most Australian employers over 100 staff run your resume through an applicant tracking
system before a person sees it. Rules that matter:

- One column, no text boxes, no tables for layout, no headers or footers holding contact
  details. The generated master resume follows this.
- File name: `Mathew Hema - Head of IT - Employer.pdf`. PDF unless they ask for Word.
- Mirror the exact job title from the posting in your headline line for that application.
- Use the posting's own phrases for the top five requirements. The board's re-score shows
  which of your keywords it found; the tailoring notes say which to add.
- Dates in the same format throughout. Month and year.

### The cover letter (280 to 380 words)

Four paragraphs. The board writes this for you; check it against this structure:

1. Role, employer, the remote statement, and one line on why this employer.
2. The pattern statement, then three proof points with numbers.
3. The two or three requirements they emphasised, each answered with a specific example.
4. What happens next. One sentence. Sign off with name, email, city, time zone.

No adjectives about yourself. No "passionate". Every claim carries a number or a named
outcome.

### The screening questions

Have these ready in the board before you apply, because ATS forms ask them cold:

- **Right to work.** State it exactly as the employer phrases it. If you are a citizen say
  "Australian citizen, no sponsorship required."
- **Location and hours.** The remote statement, plus your willingness to travel (state a
  number: "quarterly travel to Sydney is fine").
- **Salary.** Ask for their range first. If forced, give a range whose floor is your floor
  and whose ceiling is 15 percent above it. Full-time: AUD 160,000 base plus super is your
  stated floor; for a CIO title at 200 plus staff the market is 190,000 to 240,000 plus super.
  Contract: AUD 900 to 1,300 per day depending on scope. Fractional: AUD 8,000 to 15,000 a
  month for one to two days a week.
- **Notice period.** Four weeks unless your contract says otherwise. Check it.
- **Why are you leaving?** A reason about scope, not grievance. "I was brought in to deliver
  a security uplift, an integration programme and a new e-commerce channel. Those are done or
  in steady state, and I want a larger remit."
- **Why remote?** Answer with evidence, not preference: "I have led distributed teams for
  most of my career and I am more productive with a working window than with a commute. I
  travel when the work needs a room."

### Volume and quality

Five tailored applications a week beats twenty generic ones. At senior level the hiring
manager reads the letter. A letter that quotes their own posting back to them with proof gets
a screening call; a template does not.

**This week:** prepare the screening answers once in `templates/interview-bank.md`, then
paste them per role in the board so each application is a copy-and-tune, not a rewrite.

## 4. Channels, ranked by expected yield

| Channel | Response rate you should expect | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Referral into a role | 30 to 50 percent screen rate | Ask former colleagues at Crawford, Good Shepherd, ISV, PWG and board contacts. One ask a day. |
| Specialist IT recruiters (Peoplebank, Paxus, Talent, Hays, Robert Half, Michael Page, Hudson) | 20 to 40 percent will take a call | They hold lane A and C roles that never get posted. Register once, then keep them warm fortnightly. |
| Direct to hiring manager after applying | 15 to 25 percent reply | A four-line LinkedIn message the day you apply. Template in `templates/outreach.md`. |
| EthicalJobs and NFP boards | 10 to 20 percent screen rate | Your NFP record (BirdLife, Good Shepherd, board role) is a real advantage here. |
| SEEK and LinkedIn applications with a tailored letter | 8 to 15 percent screen rate | Volume channel. Tailor or do not bother. |
| Aggregators (Adzuna, Jooble, Jora) | 5 to 10 percent | Same postings as above, caught earlier. |
| Global remote boards (Himalayas, Remotive, WWR, RemoteOK) | 2 to 5 percent | Long tail. Let the tool watch them; apply only to explicit APAC or worldwide roles. |
| Cold company boards | 2 to 5 percent | Worth it only for companies you would move for. |

Funnel maths for a senior remote search: about 40 quality applications produce 8 screening
calls, 3 full interview loops, and 1 offer. At five applications a week that is eight to ten
weeks to an offer, and that assumes tailoring and outreach on every one. Track this in the
board's header (applied this week, response rate, interviewing) and adjust the mix, not the
effort, when the numbers drift.

**This week:** register with three specialist recruiters and send five referral asks.

| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| 0 | Apply. Same day, send the hiring manager or recruiter note (templates/outreach.md). |
| 7 | Follow-up one. Short, adds one new piece of evidence, asks for the timeline. |

| 21 | Close it in your head. Move the card to Closed unless they have replied. Do not send a third message. |

After an interview: thank-you note within 24 hours that references one specific thing from
the conversation and restates one proof point they seemed to care about.

## 6. LinkedIn set-up for a remote search

- **Location:** "Greater Melbourne Area" is fine for lane A. Recruiters filter by country
  more than city. Do not fake a location.
- **Open to Work:** turn it on for recruiters only, job titles as listed in section 1,
  location types Remote and Hybrid, locations Australia plus Sydney, Brisbane, Canberra,
  Perth, and Auckland, start date "flexible".
- **Headline:** `Head of IT / CIO | Remote from Melbourne | Essential Eight and ISO 27001 |
  Microsoft 365, Salesforce, NetSuite | NFP, financial services, insurance`. Recruiters search
  headlines; put the words they type.
- **About:** the pattern statement, the three proof points, the remote statement, the sectors,
  and a plain "open to Head of IT, IT Director and CIO roles, remote or hybrid across
  Australia and APAC". Eight lines.
- **Featured:** nothing that identifies BirdLife internal work. A short case-study post about
  Essential Eight uplift in an NFP, written generically, is fine.
- **Activity:** one comment a day on posts by Australian CIOs, recruiters and NFP technology
  leaders. It costs ten minutes and it is the cheapest visibility you can buy.

**This week:** update headline, About and Open to Work. Then leave it alone.

## 7. Remote interviews

A typical senior loop: recruiter or HR screen (30 minutes), hiring manager (45 to 60),
panel with peers and a direct report (60), then either a presentation or a case (60, often
a 90-day plan), then references and a CEO or board-level conversation for CIO roles.

### Questions you will get, and where your answers come from

| Question | Source in your record |
|---|---|
| How do you lead a team you do not sit with? | Crawford APAC: fourteen years reporting to the Global CIO in the USA, teams in the UK and India; regional DR programme delivered inside tolerances. |
| Tell me about a time you inherited a mess. | Partners Wealth Group (sole ICT, Salesforce MVP to core system, E8 ML3 in a regulated business) or 9 Yards (32 years of technology consolidated in three months). |
| How do you decide what to spend on? | Good Shepherd: reduced ICT cost every quarter while maintaining SLAs; owned all vendor sourcing. BirdLife: every investment justified against organisational outcomes. |
| Security: where do you start? | Essential Eight gap assessment, prioritised roadmap, board reporting. Done at PWG, MIPS and now BirdLife. ISO 27001 at Crawford. |
| How do you work with a board or executive that does not speak technology? | Board member at Dandenong Community and Learning Centre; executive committee member at Crawford; board security reporting at BirdLife. |
| What would you do in your first 90 days? | Use the plan below. |
| Why remote, and how do we know it works? | Section 3 answer plus the Crawford evidence. |
| What are you not good at? | Pick something real and bounded, with what you do about it. Do not say "I work too hard". |

Full STAR answers are in `templates/interview-bank.md`. Rehearse them out loud, on camera,
once each. The camera is the interview; practise the medium.

### The 90-day plan (when they ask for one)

1. **Days 1 to 30, understand:** stakeholder interviews (executive, each function head, the
   team, top five vendors), inventory of systems, contracts, licences and security posture,
   read the last two audits and the risk register, one quick win the business feels.
2. **Days 31 to 60, decide:** gap assessment against Essential Eight and the business
   strategy, cost baseline, team and vendor model options, a prioritised twelve-month roadmap
   with costs, presented to the executive.
3. **Days 61 to 90, start:** the first two roadmap items in delivery, security baseline
   controls in place, service metrics reported monthly, a budget the CFO trusts.

Present it as three slides and a one-page summary. Employers are not buying the plan; they
are buying evidence that you have done it before, so name where each step was done.

### Remote interview mechanics

- Camera at eye level, light in front of you, plain background, wired headset. Test the
  link ten minutes before with the same device.
- Have the posting, your letter and your proof points open on a second screen.
- Ask two questions that show you have thought about running their IT remotely: "How does
  the team work today, where are they, and what does the executive expect to see from IT
  each month?"
- Close by asking about timeline and the next step. Write the thank-you note that evening.

## 9. Hygiene and risk

- Never run the job search from BirdLife equipment, email or Microsoft 365. Personal laptop,
  personal Gmail (which the board already uses), personal LinkedIn.
- Do not mention BirdLife internal specifics (vendors, incidents, costs) in letters or
  interviews. Describe outcomes generically.
- Scam signals on remote boards: pay to apply, equipment purchase before start, interviews
  only over chat, a company whose domain was registered last month, salary far above market
  for the title. The tool cannot detect these; you can in one minute.
- Give referees notice before you list them, tell them which role, and refresh them every
  time. Referee fatigue kills late-stage processes.
- Keep the current employer relationship clean. A remote role will still ask why you are
  leaving and may back-channel in the Melbourne NFP sector, which is small.

targeting, not effort. Change the resume lead, the title band or the lane before changing
the volume.

## 11. Decision rules

- **Apply** when the role is in your band, the location policy is explicit or plausibly AU
  or APAC, and the posting names at least three things you have actually done.
- **Skip** when it is hybrid Melbourne (that is a local role), US or UK hours only, or below
  your floor with no path to it.
- **Withdraw** when the process reveals the role is not what was posted, when they cannot
  state the remote arrangement in writing, or when the hiring manager cannot describe what
  success looks like in twelve months.
- **Negotiate** always, once, on one thing that matters most to you (base, title, days
  remote, start date). Ask, then stop talking.
- **Accept** when the written offer matches the verbal one, the remote arrangement is in the
  contract, and you have spoken to at least one person who would report to you.
