# Interview bank

STAR answers built only from the resume record. Each is 60 to 90 seconds spoken. Rehearse on
camera once each. Adjust numbers only if you have better ones.

## Remote and distributed leadership

**How do you lead a team you do not sit with?**

Situation: At Crawford & Company I ran ICT for Asia Pacific for fourteen years, reporting to
the Global CIO in the USA, with teams and counterparts in the UK and India.
Task: Keep regional service levels, deliver a regional disaster recovery programme and the
Asia Pacific data loss prevention rollout, and report to a CIO nine time zones away.
Action: Fixed cadences (weekly regional stand-up, monthly metrics to the Global CIO),
ITIL-based service reporting so nobody had to be in the room to see performance, decision
rights written down so the team did not wait for me overnight, and travel when a room was
needed.
Result: Delivered the DR programme inside project tolerances, ISO 27001 processes and the DLP
programme across the region, and supported growth from 165 to over 1,000 staff with about 10
percent cost reduction and 10 percent productivity improvement year on year.

**Why remote?**

I am more productive with a working window than a commute, and I have evidence it works: the
Crawford years above, and at BirdLife I am integrating Salesforce, Microsoft 365, NetSuite
and Asana with a team of four who are not all in one place. I travel when the work needs a
room. I am asking for remote because it is how I already work, not as a lifestyle preference.

## Inheriting a mess

**Tell me about a time you took over a fragmented function.**

Situation: Partners Wealth Group, a regulated financial services business with advice,
paraplanning, SMSF audit, lending and finance functions, and ICT that was essentially one
person and a managed service provider with a Salesforce MVP nobody trusted.
Task: Sole accountability for ICT, reporting to the C-suite and board.
Action: Wrote a three-year technology plan tied to the business strategy, implemented
Essential Eight Maturity Level 3 processes, matured Salesforce from MVP into the core
business process system with internal and external developers, consolidated and renegotiated
the SaaS estate where it duplicated, and ran delivery in two-week sprints with monthly budget
reporting.
Result: A regulated business with a security baseline it could show its auditors, a core
platform staff used, and a vendor model with less duplication and lower cost.

Alternative: 9 Yards, where I consolidated 32 years of accumulated technology across service
delivery, infrastructure and security into one operating model in three months and handed it
over with a plan.

## Security

**Where do you start on security?**

With a gap assessment against Essential Eight, a prioritised roadmap costed for the
executive, and a reporting line to the board. I have done that three times: Partners Wealth
Group and Medical Indemnity Protection Society to Maturity Level 3, and BirdLife Australia now,
with a SaaS and identity overlay because most of the estate is not on-premises. Before that,
ISO 27001 processes and the DLP programme, including laptop and removable media encryption,
across Asia Pacific at Crawford.

**How do you get a board to fund security?**

Speak in their risk language. At BirdLife the roadmap goes to the board as a prioritised
remediation list with what each item reduces and what it costs. As a board member at
Dandenong Community and Learning Centre I sit on the other side of that table, so I know what
lands: three numbers, one decision, no acronyms.

## Cost and vendors

**How do you decide what to spend on?**

Every investment is justified against an organisational outcome, and I report monthly. At
Good Shepherd Microfinance I reduced ICT cost every quarter through a cloud transformation
while keeping SLAs, and owned all vendor sourcing and contracts. At Cheetham Salt I
transitioned the managed service provider and reduced communications cost across contracts,
LAN, WAN, telephony and the mobile fleet. At Crawford the budget was $5M plus a year with a
10 percent reduction target every year, and we hit it while the business grew.

**Tell me about managing a managed service provider.**

Set the model first: what is theirs, what is ours, what the SLA says, how it is reported.
Then run to it. I have transitioned MSPs at Cheetham Salt and managed them at PWG and now
BirdLife. The failure mode is treating the MSP as the IT department; the fix is a
business-facing vendor manager, which is what I set up at Cheetham.

## Delivery

**Tell me about a large delivery you owned end to end.**

Good Shepherd Microfinance: an automated end-to-end online lending platform, from inception
to live operations, across PaaS, IaaS and SaaS in multiple cloud environments, then the
lessons reused to rebuild the No Interest Loans Scheme platform in a separate cloud
environment. Along the way I rebuilt the ICT team to fit the new operating model, centralised
data and reporting, and led the security and policy uplift.

**How do you run delivery day to day?**

Two-week sprints with the business in the room (PWG), PRINCE2 and PMBOK where the size
warrants it, ITIL for service. I introduced ITIL, PMBOK, PRINCE2 and Agile into Crawford's
regional business and embedded the metrics to the Global CIO.

## People

**How have you built teams?**

From one to thirty. At Good Shepherd I rebuilt the team to fit the new operating model and
productivity went up while BAU and SLA compliance held. At Independent Schools Victoria I led
eight across development, security, infrastructure, research and relationship management,
five technical and three stakeholder-facing. At Crawford I went from service desk to regional
lead, so I know what the people in the team are dealing with.

**What are you not good at?**

Pick one real, bounded thing and what you do about it. Example: I move to a plan fast and can
under-explain the why to people who need more context; I now write the one-page rationale
before the plan and test it with a sceptic first.

## Questions to ask them

1. Where is the team today, and how does the executive expect to see IT performance each month?
2. What did the last person in this role get right, and what did they run out of road on?
3. What would make you say in twelve months that this hire worked?
4. What is the security posture today, honestly, and who owns it at board level?
5. What is the remote arrangement in writing, and how often do you expect me in a room?
