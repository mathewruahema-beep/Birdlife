How to recognise an AI opportunity

Use this while scoring — the signals map to the feasibility dimensions

Strong signals

  • High volume, same shape. The task repeats weekly or daily — 410 of 425 Zeus cases arrive as free-text email.
  • Language in, language out. Reading, classifying, summarising, drafting — AI's home ground.
  • An easy human check. The output is quick to verify, so review costs minutes, not the task itself.
  • Data already reachable. Salesforce, NetSuite, Microsoft 365, Asana and Zapier are all connected — no digitisation project first.
  • A person stays in the loop. AI drafts, a human sends.

Red flags

  • Decisions about people — hiring, performance, member sanctions — with no human review.
  • Sensitive data leaving governed systems. Member, donor or payroll data pasted into unapproved tools.
  • Rare and irreversible. A task done twice a year with high stakes — the setup never pays back and the risk never shrinks.
  • Tacit knowledge. The rules live in one person's head and nowhere else — document first, automate second.
  • The demo is the easy 80%. If the hard 20% still needs the expert every time, the saving is smaller than it looks.

Guardrails for every pilot

  • Human review before it reaches anyone outside — members, donors, volunteers, the public.
  • Keep data in governed systems. Work through approved connectors, not copy-paste; respect the Privacy Act handling of member data.
  • Bound the pilot and measure. One queue, one month, a before/after number — then decide.
  • Make provenance visible. Log what AI touched, the same principle the ICT dashboard applies to scope.
  • Name an owner. An opportunity with no owner is a Parked item wearing a different chip.
